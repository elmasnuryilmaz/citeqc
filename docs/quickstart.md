# Quick start

This page runs the whole workflow on a small **synthetic** CITE-seq dataset
that ships with citeqc. Nothing to download, and it takes a few seconds.

!!! warning "Simulated data"
    The example dataset is generated by
    [`citeqc.datasets.synthetic`](api.md#citeqc.datasets.synthetic). It is not
    real data and supports no biological conclusion. It exists so that every
    antibody's behaviour is **known by construction** and you can check that
    citeqc recovers it.

## The example dataset

3,150 T cells from 4 simulated donors x 2 cell types (`CD4 T`, `CD8 T`), with
15 antibodies and 25 genes as raw UMI counts. The design, also written to
`design.csv`:

| Design | Antibodies (cognate gene) | Built so that |
|---|---|---|
| coupled | CD3 (*CD3E*), CD2, CD5, CD7, CD27, CD28, CD62L (*SELL*), CD127 (*IL7R*), CD45RO (*PTPRC*) | the protein follows its transcript's per-cell state, plus protein-specific noise |
| decoupled | CD45RA (*PTPRC*), CD152 (*CTLA4*), CD154 (*CD40LG*) | the protein varies independently of its transcript |
| absent | CD19, CD20 (*MS4A1*) | B-cell markers on T cells: only ambient antibody background, so they are negative controls |
| isotype | IgG1 | no cognate gene |

Two artefacts are planted on purpose:

- donor `D4` was stained with a panel that lacks CD154 (its values are NaN);
- `groups.csv` holds pseudobulk profiles of 120 "pseudo-clonotypes" of 5 to 300
  cells drawn at random from the same cells, so no gene truly depends on group
  size.

The RNA table is a 25-gene subset of the transcriptome, so `nCount_RNA` in
`meta.csv` (the whole-library size) is larger than the row sums of `rna.csv`.

## From the command line

Write the example files:

```bash
citeqc example citeqc_example
```

Measure coupling. The inputs are raw counts, so `--normalise` applies CLR to
the antibodies and log counts per 10,000 (scaled by `nCount_RNA`) to the RNA:

```bash
citeqc couple --adt citeqc_example/adt.csv --rna citeqc_example/rna.csv \
  --meta citeqc_example/meta.csv --normalise --show 5
```

## Reading the result

- **Coupled and decoupled markers separate cleanly.** All nine coupled
  antibodies have `median_rho` between 0.35 and 0.63 and their cognate gene
  ranks first among the 25 genes. The decoupled and absent ones sit near zero.
- **`mechanism` is an annotation, not a result.** It comes from the bundled
  table and marks a documented post-transcriptional mechanism. Here CD45RO
  carries `splice_isoform` yet couples well, because the simulation made it
  follow *PTPRC*. In real data, use the annotation to interpret a low value,
  not to predict one.
- **`spec_percentile` is only meaningful when the coupling is real.** For a
  marker whose correlations with every gene are near zero, where the cognate
  gene falls among them is noise: CD154 ranks 2nd of 25 with
  `cognate_rho` = 0.07. Read `spec_percentile` together with `cognate_rho`.
- **`n_strata` exposes the missing panel.** CD154 is computed from 6 strata,
  not 8, because donor D4 never measured it, which is what
  `panel_consistency` reports. For CD19 and CD20 some strata drop out because
  their RNA is almost always zero, so the correlation is undefined there.
- **CD45RA comes out slightly negative, not zero.** That is a property of the
  per-cell ADT normalisation, not of the coupling measure. CLR centres each
  cell on the mean of all its antibodies, and that mean includes CD45RO, which
  follows *PTPRC* by construction; on this 15-antibody panel, CD45RA inherits
  a small negative dependence on *PTPRC*. Leaving CD45RO out of the CLR brings
  CD45RA back to about zero. Any normalisation that divides by a per-cell
  antibody total behaves the same way, and the effect shrinks as the panel
  grows.
- **The size artefact is pure statistics.** The pseudo-clonotypes are random
  samples of the same cells, yet 80% of genes drift the same way with group
  size, because the log of a mean over few cells is pulled down more than the
  log of a mean over many. Averaging exactly 5 cells per group removes it.

## Next steps

Replace the example files with your own tables (cells in rows; see the
[CLI reference](cli.md)) or call the [Python API](api.md) directly. If your
data are in a `.h5mu` file, [`io.from_mudata`](api.md#citeqc.io.from_mudata)
returns the three tables `measure` expects.
